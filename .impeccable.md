## Design Context

### Users

myDAUST serves students and university staff. For first-time account activation, students may be unfamiliar with the portal and are completing the flow in person while registrars handle a busy check-in desk. The immediate job is to verify the right student, pair the student's browser with a named staff approval, and reach password setup in a few minutes without email access.

### Brand Personality

Trustworthy, calm, and purposeful. The interface should feel like a modern university service: authoritative enough for identity work, humane in its language, and composed under operational pressure.

### Aesthetic Direction

Use the established DAUST visual system: navy and deep navy as the institutional foundation, orange only for focused action and emphasis, steel-tinted neutrals, Saira for display type, and Montserrat for body copy. Favor a refined, light-first, utilitarian layout with strong hierarchy and restrained motion. Preserve the existing token-based dark mode. Avoid generic gradient-heavy SaaS styling, decorative glass effects, or playful treatments that weaken trust.

### Design Principles

1. Optimize for a three-minute, first-time activation at a busy registrar desk.
2. Reveal one decision at a time: identify, pair, approve, then set the password.
3. Keep identity language precise and reassuring; never expose birth dates or bearer credentials to staff.
4. Make every state recoverable and explicit, including waiting, expiry, mismatch, and retry.
5. Meet WCAG AA expectations with keyboard access, visible focus, semantic status updates, reduced-motion respect, and no color-only meaning.
