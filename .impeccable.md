## Design Context

### Users

myDAUST serves students and university staff. For first-time account activation, students may be unfamiliar with the portal and are completing the flow in person during a busy school-year launch. The immediate job is to use an individually issued activation card, confirm the card's Student ID and date-of-birth binding, and reach password setup in a few minutes without email access or a staff approval screen.

### Brand Personality

Trustworthy, calm, and purposeful. The interface should feel like a modern university service: authoritative enough for identity work, humane in its language, and composed under operational pressure.

### Aesthetic Direction

Use the established DAUST visual system: navy and deep navy as the institutional foundation, orange only for focused action and emphasis, steel-tinted neutrals, Saira for display type, and Montserrat for body copy. Favor a refined, light-first, utilitarian layout with strong hierarchy and restrained motion. Preserve the existing token-based dark mode. Avoid generic gradient-heavy SaaS styling, decorative glass effects, or playful treatments that weaken trust.

### Design Principles

1. Optimize for a three-minute, self-service first-time activation during busy check-in.
2. Reveal one decision at a time: enter card details, then set the password.
3. Keep identity language precise and reassuring; activation cards are private one-time credentials.
4. Make every state recoverable and explicit, including expiry, mismatch, and reissue.
5. Meet WCAG AA expectations with keyboard access, visible focus, semantic status updates, reduced-motion respect, and no color-only meaning.
